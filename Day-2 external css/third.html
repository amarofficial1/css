Project Hail Mary

Project Hail Mary is a science-fiction story by Andy Weir about Ryland Grace, a middle-school science teacher who wakes up with amnesia aboard a deep-space starship. As his memory slowly returns, he realizes he is the lone survivor of a desperate mission to save Earth from Astrophage, a parasitic microorganism that is dimming the Sun and threatening the planet with extinction. While investigating the Tau Ceti system, Grace meets Rocky, a rock-like, five-limbed alien engineer whose home star is facing the exact same threat. The two form an unlikely and heartwarming partnership, combining their scientific expertise to discover a natural predator called taumoeba that clears the parasite and saves both of their worlds.

Alchemist

Written by Brazilian author Paulo Coelho in 1988, The Alchemist is a philosophical and spiritual fable about Santiago, a young Andalusian shepherd boy who embarks on a journey from Spain to the Egyptian pyramids after repeatedly dreaming of a hidden treasure. Along his quest, Santiago is guided by an old king, works for a crystal merchant, crosses the Sahara Desert, meets a wise alchemist, and learns to listen to his heart. Through these transformative experiences, the novel explores the core theme of a "Personal Legend"—the true purpose or dream a person is meant to fulfill—teaching that the universe conspires to help those who have the courage to follow their path, and that the real treasure is often the wisdom gained along the journey itself.

The Murder of Roger Ackroyd

Published by Agatha Christie in 1926, The Murder of Roger Ackroyd is a landmark detective novel narrated by Dr. James Sheppard in the English village of King's Abbot. The plot follows wealthy widower Roger Ackroyd, who is stabbed to death in his locked study shortly after discovering that his late fiancée, Mrs. Ferrars, was being driven to suicide by an anonymous blackmailer. As the retired Belgian detective Hercule Poirot investigates the household secrets and forged alibis, he unmasks the killer in one of mystery fiction's most famous plot twists: Dr. Sheppard himself is the blackmailer and murderer, who used a dictaphone to fake the time of death before penning his final confession.
